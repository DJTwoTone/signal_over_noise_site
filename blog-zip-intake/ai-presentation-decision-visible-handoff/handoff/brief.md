## Commercial question

Can a reader see the difference between a presentation that sounds polished and one that makes the requested decision easy to understand?

## Audience

Korean and international teams preparing high-stakes English presentations for investors, executives, customers, or partners.

## Working title

> AI Can Make Your Presentation Fluent Without Making the Decision Visible

## Hook

> AI can make a presentation fluent without making the decision visible.

## Practical promise

Help the reader notice when a polished deck explains the situation beautifully but leaves the audience unsure what they are being asked to decide, approve, fund, or do.

## Core point

Fluent wording is not the same as a visible decision. A presentation can contain accurate context, elegant transitions, and professional language while burying the actual ask under background, evidence, and process.

## Example needed

Use a clearly conceptual contrast:

- Buried ask: “This proposal explores several options for improving regional expansion over the next two quarters.”
- Visible ask: “We recommend Option B for the next two quarters. Today we need approval to begin the pilot.”

Label the example as illustrative, not client work.

## Draft shape

1. State the fluent-but-not-decision-visible tension.
2. Show the conceptual before/after.
3. Explain three places where polished work can still hide judgment: neutral titles, equal-weight options, and evidence that arrives before the ask.
4. Give a short decision-visibility check.
5. Close with the idea that human review checks the decision path, not only the wording, and bridge to the Free Presentation Diagnostic.
