## Why this is the next article

The current repository’s series manifest explicitly names this as Part 4, after **Make the Actor Obvious** and before **Build Recovery Points Into Spoken Language**. It continues the same practical problem: report-style writing often optimizes for visual variety and compactness, while spoken language needs a few stable anchors the audience can recognize as the talk moves.

## Strategy

- **Audience:** professionals preparing English or international-facing presentations, especially people converting reports, research papers, proposals, or AI-assisted drafts into spoken English.
- **Audience question:** “Is repeating the same word in a presentation bad English, or can repetition make my point easier to follow?”
- **Practical promise:** show when to repeat the important word, where to repeat it, and how to avoid turning repetition into filler.
- **Business purpose:** trust-building, search visibility, answer visibility, and diagnostic conversion.
- **Proof available:** general presentation-language judgment and illustrative examples.
- **Proof missing:** none required. Examples must remain clearly conceptual, not client stories or measured outcomes.
- **Main risk:** do not imply that repetition is always good, that international audiences all process language identically, or that the diagnostic includes a rewrite or live call by default.

## SEO / answer intent

- **Search intent:** informational and service-support; help someone improve spoken English clarity in a presentation.
- **Primary keyword:** `repetition in presentations`
- **Secondary keywords:** `presentation repetition`, `repeated words in English presentation`, `spoken English clarity`, `English presentation tips`, `presentation language`
- **Short answer:** Repeating an important word helps a listening audience keep track of the point, especially when they cannot reread the sentence. Repeat the anchor term when it carries the meaning; do not repeat every sentence or use repetition to hide a vague idea.
- **Answer targets:** simple rule, before/after contrast, four useful moments for repetition, five-minute editing check.
