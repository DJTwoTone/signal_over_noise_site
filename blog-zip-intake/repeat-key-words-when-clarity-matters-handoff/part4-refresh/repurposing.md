# Repurposing — Repeat Key Words When Clarity Matters

## LinkedIn post

A listener does not get to reread your sentence.

That is why repeating the important word can be clearer than replacing it with three synonyms.

On a page, “approval time,” “delay,” “bottleneck,” and “constraint” may look like stylish variation.

In a presentation, your audience may wonder whether you mean four different problems.

Try this instead:

> The main issue is approval time. Approval time is holding up the launch. If we reduce approval time first, the rest of the schedule becomes easier to manage.

Repetition is useful when it gives the audience a stable anchor.

It is not useful when it repeats a vague idea or hides the fact that you have not chosen the point yet.

Before your next presentation, circle the word that carries each section. Put that word back into the transition and the close.

Speaking is not an essay with fewer pages.

## Short email

Subject: Repeating yourself may be helping

If you are polishing an English presentation, you may be trying too hard to avoid repeating the same word.

That is usually good advice for writing. It is less reliable when people are listening.

A repeated key word can help the audience keep track of the point while you add detail, move between sections, or make a recommendation.

The test is simple: repeat the word that carries the meaning, not the whole sentence. If you are not sure which word that is, that may be the bigger presentation problem.

## 45-second video outline

**Hook:** “Should you avoid repeating words in a presentation? Not always.”

**Point:** A reader can look back. A listener cannot. If you keep swapping the same idea for synonyms, the audience may think you have introduced new ideas.

**Example:** “Approval time is the issue. Approval time is holding up the launch. We recommend reducing approval time first.”

**Rule:** Repeat the anchor word when it carries the point. Do not repeat the whole sentence or use repetition to hide a vague idea.

**Close:** “If your deck or script is hard to follow out loud, a Free Presentation Diagnostic can help identify what to fix first.”

## Outreach follow-up

This may be useful for the presentation you mentioned. If the same idea keeps appearing under different labels, the audience may have to work out whether the terms are connected. Repeating the key word at the point, transition, and close can make the logic easier to follow.

## Workshop activity

Give participants one paragraph from a report-style presentation script.

Ask them to:

1. write the section’s main point in one phrase
2. circle the anchor word
3. identify unnecessary synonyms
4. add the anchor to one transition and the final sentence
5. read the paragraph aloud once

Debrief with one question: “Did the repeated word make the relationship clearer, or did it expose that the section has more than one point?”
