# Article brief — A Technical Result Needs Context Before a Decision

**Editorial slot:** Thursday, 2026-10-22  
**Content job:** Attract — make a specific presentation risk visible  
**Status:** Draft ready for review; unpublished  
**Content lane:** Technical Presentation Clarity; Claims and Evidence  
**Campaign question:** Can the audience tell what a technical result establishes, under which conditions, and what decision it supports?  
**Primary CTA:** None; this Attract piece teaches a practical check without a hard conversion ask.

## Strategy

- **Audience:** Korean and international teams explaining technical work to investors, executives, customers, or partners, especially when some decision makers are not specialists in the work.
- **Situation:** A slide presents a test result, but leaves the audience to guess what was tested, under which conditions, how it compares with a threshold or baseline, and what follows from it.
- **Audience question:** How do I present technical evidence to a nontechnical decision maker?
- **Practical promise:** Show how to pair a technical result with its test, conditions, limits, and decision implication so the audience can judge what the evidence supports.
- **Business purpose:** Build trust by demonstrating specific review judgment, answer a concrete search question, and create a useful diagnostic entry point for people preparing technical decks.
- **Proof:** A clearly labelled synthetic slide template plus an attributed reconstruction of NASA Flight Readiness Review findings from the Rogers Commission report. The historical example shows how the STS 51-C O-ring erosion and low-temperature risk was summarized during the STS 51-E review; the report gives the O-ring temperature at launch as 53°F. It is not the original slide, and it does not establish that one summary caused the later Challenger accident. Do not invent metrics, results, client findings, company names, or endorsements.
- **Example boundary:** Keep the STS 51-E review distinct from the later STS 51-L Challenger mission. Do not imply a universal test method, claim sole causation, or recommend a technical decision without the relevant source and domain review.
- **Differentiation:** The October 19 article checks whether a proposal asks for a commitment proportionate to its evidence. This article shows how to make technical evidence interpretable. The October 26 article covers labeling early customer proof and will use a separate example.
- **CTA fit:** No hard CTA, in line with the Attract slot.

## Editorial plan

- **Core argument:** A technical result does not explain itself. Before asking an audience to trust its implication, show what was tested, the conditions, the observed result, its limits, and the decision it informs.
- **Example:** A reusable synthetic slide template with placeholders: `[claim to evaluate]`; `[test and conditions]`; `[observed result and unit]`; `[comparison or threshold, if relevant]`; `[limitation]`; `[decision this evidence can inform]`. Follow it with an attributed reconstruction of the Rogers Commission account of the STS 51-E Flight Readiness Review. Label the documented facts and the article analysis separately.
- **Reader check:** Could a smart non-specialist explain what the result means, what it does not establish, and what decision it can support?
- **Outline:** Open with a result that looks persuasive but lacks context; introduce the six evidence fields; show the bracketed template; reconstruct the Commission account of the STS 51-E review; distinguish documented facts from analysis; separate observation from implication; show how to state the limitation; give a short pre-meeting review.
- **Sources:** NASA Rogers Commission, *Report of the Presidential Commission on the Space Shuttle Challenger Accident*, Volume I, Chapter VI. Attribute the historical account to the report; label the template mapping as a reconstruction and do not imply sole causation.
- **Internal links:** Link to *What I Would Flag First on a Polished Proposal Slide* for checking whether a requested commitment fits the evidence. Add a link to *Label Early Customer Proof Before It Goes on the Slide* after that article is live.
- **Repurposing:** LinkedIn text post showing the evidence fields that make a technical result reviewable, only after the article is finalized and its canonical URL is verified.
