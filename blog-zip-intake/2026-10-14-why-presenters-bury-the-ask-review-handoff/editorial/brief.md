- **Audience question:** Why can a good presenter explain the situation clearly and still leave the ask hard to find?

## Practical promise and angle

Explain why a capable presenter can cover the situation clearly and still leave the audience unsure what response is expected. Keep the piece focused on naming the room's role—decide, advise, or hear an update—and ordering the opening around that role, with only the context needed to respond.

### Differentiation required

Two live articles already cover adjacent territory: [AI Can Make Your Presentation Fluent Without Making the Decision Visible](/insights/ai-presentation-decision-visible/) covers slide-level decision visibility; [When Your English Presentation Is Clear but Not Persuasive](/insights/english-presentation-clear-but-not-persuasive/) covers a buried recommendation, softened language, and a broad persuasion checklist. Keep this new piece focused on **naming the room's role at the start: decide, advise, or receive an update**. Do not repeat the AI-fluency explanation, proposal-slide before/after, or broad persuasion checklist. A spoken opening example should make the distinction concrete.

## Draft shape

1. Open with a meeting moment: the presenter has explained the context, but the room still does not know whether to decide, advise, or listen.
2. Explain the planning gap: presenters often plan their explanation before naming the audience's role.
3. Show clearly labelled synthetic opening lines for a decision, advice request, and informational update. Use placeholders, not invented results.
4. Show a practical planning order: name the room's role, add the context needed to respond, then state the next step. If the role is still unclear, settle the meeting's purpose before polishing the opening.
5. Close with that planning prompt and link to the existing decision-visibility article for a separate slide-level review.
