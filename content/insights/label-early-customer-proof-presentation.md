In a synthetic pitch slide, the headline reads **“Customer traction validates our model.”** One operations leader has described a workflow problem, one prospective customer has agreed to evaluate the tool, and a second has agreed to a pilot that has not started.

That evidence shows a problem conversation, an evaluation, and an agreed but unstarted pilot. The headline may lead a reader to assume customers are buying, using the product, and seeing results. State what happened, which customer or workflow it covers, and where it stands. Tie any outcome claim to evidence.

## Use the verb that matches the evidence

Consider this **synthetic example**, not a real company: a team is developing a tool for operations teams that manage incoming work.

- An operations leader describes requests being copied between systems. That is a **customer conversation**. Record the problem the leader raised. Use a separate signal before saying the customer wants to buy a solution.
- One prospective customer agrees to assess the tool against its current intake process. That is an **evaluation**. Name the workflow or criteria the customer is checking.
- A second customer agrees to a limited trial for one workflow, but the team has not set a start date. Call it an **agreed pilot** and state that it has not started.
- If that customer starts the pilot and uses the tool for the workflow, report that the **pilot is underway** and the tool is **in use for a defined task**. Measure an outcome before claiming the tool improved the process.
- If the pilot customer later renews its agreement, report a **renewal** for this customer. That does not establish demand elsewhere.

Use these labels as a practical review aid. Customer relationships may move through them in a different order or skip some, so match each label to the record you have.

## Show the stage, scope, and status

For the synthetic example, the slide might read:

> **Early customer evidence**
>
> **Problem conversation:** An operations leader described difficulty tracking incoming requests across systems.
>
> **Evaluation:** One prospective customer agreed to assess the tool against its current intake workflow.
>
> **Pilot:** A second customer agreed to a limited pilot for one workflow; it has not started yet.
>
> **Outcome:** The team has not collected pilot results yet.
>
> **Next step:** Confirm the pilot start date and agree what the team will review.

*Synthetic example for illustration; not client work. No customer results are being claimed.*

The slide says the pilot is agreed but has not started, so the audience can see its status. When the pilot starts, update that line. To claim that the tool reduced handling time, compare a defined measure with a baseline, show how you collected it, and check what else changed during the pilot. A lower number alone does not show that the tool caused the change; [these five questions can help you check a data-slide claim](/insights/five-questions-data-slide/).

## Check the claim before the slide leaves the team

Ask these questions before you call the evidence “traction.” The goal is to name the activity accurately and keep the claim small enough to match what is actually true.

1. **What happened?** Was this a conversation, evaluation, agreed pilot, live use, completed trial, or renewal?
   <br>
   **Example:**
   “A prospective customer agreed to evaluate the tool for its intake workflow.”

2. **What is the scope?** Which customer, team, workflow, or period does it cover?
   <br>
   **Example:**
   “One operations team is evaluating the tool for intake processing.”

3. **What is the status?** Is it proposed, agreed, underway, or complete?
   <br>
   **Example:**
   “A second customer has agreed to a limited pilot, but it has not started yet.”

4. **What supports that wording?** Check the meeting notes, written agreement, usage record, or renewal document that matches the claim.
   <br>
   **Example:**
   “Meeting notes record that the operations leader raised duplicate entry as a problem.”

5. **Are you describing activity or an outcome?** Separate use from a claimed result such as saved time, improved quality, or wider adoption.
   <br>
   **Example:**
   “The team is using the tool for intake; its effect has not yet been measured.”

6. **What decision does this evidence support?** Ask what the next step is: another interview, a bounded pilot, or a broader rollout.
   <br>
   **Example:**
   “Ask what the team would need to test in a limited pilot before discussing a larger rollout.”

If you cannot support the wording with a clear record, mark it “to confirm” in the working draft and check it before presenting. Keep it off the slide until you can support it.

## Give the audience a clear next step

If all you have is a customer conversation, put the problem they raised on the slide and say what you plan to test next. A buyer can judge that. “Traction” leaves them guessing which customer activity you mean.

Before you present, ask a colleague to identify the activity and its status from the slide. If they cannot, make the label more precise.
